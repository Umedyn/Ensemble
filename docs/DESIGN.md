# Ensemble design

How the engine works and why it works that way. Most of the "why" comes from playtesting with a local 12B model; the decision log at the end records what went wrong and what fixed it.

## What it is

Pax Historia is AI-driven grand strategy: you play a nation, and other nations react. Ensemble keeps Pax's loop (state an action, time passes, the world responds, events happen) but at personal scale. You are one person in a group, and everyone else is an individual with wants, needs, secrets, guarded knowledge and feelings about each other. Groups and factions still exist (Mission Control, a corporate sponsor, a rescue convoy) and act collectively; the story shows how individual choices ripple out to them and how their moves land on people.

It sits between SillyTavern (rich characters, but the model holds all the state) and Pax (rich world state, but no one to know). The distinguishing features are an engine that owns the facts, secrets with owners, and a world that keeps moving on its own.

It is local-first: KoboldCpp on your own GPU by default, with OpenAI-compatible APIs (e.g. OpenRouter) as a first-class option for people without a GPU or on phones.

## Core principle: the engine owns facts, the model proposes

The model is used for judgment and prose. Everything that must stay true (where people are, who knows what, health, needs, relationships, time, clocks) lives in a JSON state that only the engine changes, after validating what the model proposed. This is what keeps a long story consistent with a small model, and it's why most fixes in the decision log are engine-side guards rather than prompt wording.

## Content principles

Ensemble doesn't police content beyond what the law requires or what a service it depends on requires. Adults choose their own stories, and tasteful 18+ roleplay is a supported use. Mature content is opt-in: a world declares a rating, the player sets a maximum, and the lower of the two selects the narrator's guidance.

One hard line holds in every mode: no sexual content involving minors. When mature content is on and anyone in the scene is under 18 or has no age set, the engine forces fade-to-black guidance and drops romantic or sexual intents involving them. This belongs in the engine, not the prompt or the provider: inference engines like KoboldCpp and llama.cpp don't filter anything and don't know who is in a scene. Ensemble does, because it owns the cast and the scene. (Planned: ROADMAP › Content settings.)

## State

`Engine.newGame(scenario)` builds:

- `characters[id]`: sheet (name, pronouns, role, traits, want, secret, guard, voice, skills), `needs` (0–1), `status` (health, location), `tags` ([{text, set, until, reason}]), `mood` ({text, turn, reason}; non-player only), `relations[otherId]` (−100..100: how this person feels about the other), `tier` (`local` | `secondary`), `pinned`, `lastSeen` (turn), `agenda` (`{goal, steps: [{text, contact}], hours, visibility, step, progress, due, done, followUp}`, or `null` for routine; non-player only), and for the player `attitudeNotes` (latest reason per person)
- `locations`, `world` (key → short status), `factions` (name, desc, stance), `clocks` (pressures with `value/max/perHour`, `fireText`, `fireWorld`, follow-up `then` clocks)
- `facts[]`: `{ text, about[], known_by[] | ['all'], weight 1–3, turn, chapter }`
- `chapters[]`, `recent` (last scene), `log` (display only), `news`, `pending` (the last turn awaiting memory), `undo` (the whole state from before the last action), `tierLast` (when regional/world events last happened)

## The turn

0. **Promote for the action** (`promoteForAction`, before the turn's start snapshot): the people present and anyone the action names become local.
1. **Commit the previous turn** (`commitPending`). Run the update step on the scene as it actually played out, then update cast tiers (`updateTiers`). If the player stepped in after beat *k*, first rebuild the turn from its starting snapshot: re-apply the player's own move, advance time to the midpoint between beat *k* and *k+1*, and give the update step only beats 1..*k*. Then close a chapter if due.
2. **Roll event tiers** (`tiersDue`): are a regional and/or world event due, given elapsed time, scenario rates and cooldowns?
3. **Intent call** (model): judge the player's action (roll needed? skill, difficulty, move); one intent per present character (action, says, reveals, visibility, move); events for the span (local, plus regional/world only if due, with effects). Sees secrets and private knowledge of present characters, a brief (want, secret) of offstage local characters, and one line (role, place, want) per secondary character.
4. **Resolve** (engine): cancel moves away from the person the action names (unless the player states the move or names someone at the destination); gate reveals by guard; detect leaks; roll d20 + 2×skill vs 8/12/16 with injury and exhaustion penalties; apply moves; apply events and their effects; hidden events become private facts.
5. **Advance time**: needs tick (or settle, for downtime), clocks advance and may fire (adding follow-up clocks; a `fireHarm` clock hurts people, a `repeat` clock starts again), region tags that events added fade after `Eng.REGION_TAG_HOURS` (72).
6. **Narrate call** (model): a scene frame (starts where/with whom, ends where, who left or arrived), status with pronouns, the player's attitude toward those present, resolved outcomes (covert ones only as "out of sight"), and beat instructions. The narrator never sees secrets.
7. **Clean the prose**: drop repeated paragraphs, cut back to the last full sentence, drop trailing paragraphs that are the player's own action, regenerate once if it walks the player into another room.
7b. **Fill a short scene, then settle the clock** (about an hour, a few hours only): if the last beat is before half the step, one continuation call asks for 1–2 closing beats (same cleanup; dropped if it relocates the player). Then time is wound back to the last beat plus 10% of the step, unless a pressure clock fired during the step. The other steps keep their full span.
8. **Store as pending** with beats, the starting snapshot, everything the update step will need, and `narration` (the turn's system prompt, step, and the time state before and after the step) for Reroll.

A normal turn is three model calls (intent, narrate, update), plus an occasional chapter summary or narration retry.

**Suggested actions** (`Eng.suggestActions`, outside the turn): one call with the shared header and a user message of the narrator-side `now` block (no secrets, no one else's feelings), the scene up to the player's step-in point, and the player character's own `want`. The engine keeps at most 4 short distinct strings (`cleanSuggestions`) and changes no state; the page shows them as buttons that fill the action box. It routes with the intent step (`LLM.ROUTE_AS`), and isn't recorded, since it never changes the game.

**Generated starts** (task 4-2, `js/startgen.js`, before a game exists): a job (plain JSON: region, note, the character or a line, and each step's checked output) runs steps in order: `player` (only for a line), `rooms`, `plan`, `cast` (one call per person, or one call for all when the World building backend isn't KoboldCpp; `onePass` is fixed when the job starts), `ties`, `extras`, `opening`. Every call has the same system message (`genHeader`: setting, genre, rules, regions, groups, names already used) and a user message with the region, the player's note, a one-line-per-item summary of what earlier steps made (secrets only in the ties step) and the task. Each step's checker (`StartGen.check`) returns checked data or a problem in words; a problem is named in the next try (up to 3 tries, on top of the JSON retry). Checkers fix what a retry would likely get wrong again (rule 1): connections made two-way, unreachable rooms linked to the starting room, clashing ids renamed, names resolved to ids, values clamped (feelings toward the player to ±25: they have only just met), the player's own feelings and private facts about the player dropped (rule 3), a couple or family named as one minor person turned into a group, prose cut at a sentence or word, an age on the end of a role moved to the sheet, the people the opening shows (its `with_you`, or else everyone it names) moved to the player's room, the opening's player ending trimmed. They reject what can't be fixed: too few rooms or people, a reused name, a person sharing the player's surname or tied to the player in their role (the player's past is theirs), a sheet, opening or fact that names someone from the world's authored cast, a described player with no skills, a person without a want or secret, an opening or fact that shares 4+ content words with someone's secret, and a pressure (shown in the Situation panel) that names someone and shares 2+ word stems with their secret. The page saves the job after every step (and after every person), so a failed or closed run resumes there; `clearFrom` redoes one step and those after it. `StartGen.scenario` turns a finished job into a start plus character files and validates them exactly like authored ones.

**Review** (task 4-3): a finished job is edited in place. `setField` checks one field (types, limits, unique names, a secret for everyone but the player) and stores nothing on failure; `reviewProblems` runs the full validation plus the opening, fact and pressure leak checks, and Play and Save wait until it is empty. `rerollPerson` reruns the person step for one plan entry with the others as context (the old sheet left out of the prompt) and keeps the id, so ties, minor people and the opening still point at them; it warns that those may now be stale rather than redoing them. `rerollRoom` rewrites one room's name and description (id and connections kept); `redoOnly` reruns the ties, extras or opening step without clearing the others. The map preview is the game's own local map drawn against a throwaway game of the start. Secrets, private facts and notes stay behind a spoiler toggle that is off by default.

**Generated sites** (task 4-4, `js/sites.js`, inside the turn): after the intent call and before resolution, `Sites.forIntent` reads the player's `new_place` (or an unknown `move_to`). It is a request, never a command: the engine picks its region (named after a region: that one, overruling the model, `site region`; playtest-r2-ridge), and when the player's words set out for a region by name that the model's move doesn't reach, it makes that region the new place (`Sites.regionHeadedFor`; playtest-r2-drive-to-ridge) and refuses one the player's words don't name, one too far away, one behind a locked door (checked before any call), or one past `maxSites`; a known site just becomes the move's target. Building uses the turn's own header as the system message (the cache holds) and StartGen's retry loop: a layout call (skipped for a library map) and a people call, each with its checker. The site is added to the live state **and** to the turn's start-of-turn copy, so a step-in keeps it. The player then moves through the normal rules (route link, locked doors). Rooms made in play never enter the header (rule 7): the intent message lists the current site's rooms and the places the story knows of, and "Ways from here" shows routes as "the way to …". Visits are counted after the moves; notable facts at commit keep a site; `Sites.fade` returns faded sites to stubs. Debug lines: `site built`, `site why`, `site note`, `site refused`, `site faded`, `site kept`. **Base maps and real places** (task 4-5, `js/geo.js`, DOM-free): `Geo.toPixel`/`toLonLat` for a map with `projection` and `bounds`; shapes in pieces (`Geo.pieces`: a polygon, polygons or nothing for a point), labels at `Geo.anchor` (inside the biggest piece), `Geo.simplify` (Douglas–Peucker on rings), `Geo.clip` (Sutherland–Hodgman to the image), `Geo.neighbours` (a corner within a tolerance of the other's edge, after a box test; on the source's degrees, where shared borders are exact), and `Geo.importRegions`/`importPlaces` for the tool. `MapDraw.worldSvg` keeps text, badges and pins the same size on screen by scaling them with the view (`k = viewBox width / 900`), shows a region's label only where it fits, and draws `places` as dots, named when zoomed in or `notable`. `Prompts.regionOutline` (R-1; it replaced 4-5's `regionFocus`) chooses the regions in full once more than 24 exist; it depends only on the scenario, so the header is stable. `tools/basemap.mjs` reads Natural Earth's zip and baseline TIFF itself and writes JPEG with `jpeg-js` (BSD-3-Clause, a dev dependency); no network in tests (`io` stands in for the raster, the GeoJSON and fetch).

**Generated regions** (R-6, `js/sites.js`, inside the turn, just before generated sites): `Sites.regionForIntent` reads the player's `new_region` through `Sites.regionWanted` (a request: refused unless the player's words name it, the parent is known and reachable by `Engine.regionTrip`, the name is new and the world is under `maxRegions`; a faded stub of the same name is rebuilt instead). `Sites.buildRegion` makes one `worldbuild (region: …)` call through StartGen's retry loop with `Sites.checkRegionLore` (length, a secret's words, a pending timeline event's words, a cast name). `Sites.addRegion` puts it in the state's `scenario.regions` (and the turn's start-of-turn copy) with `generated: true` and a route of the world's `routeKind` from the player's region; it then sets the intent's `new_place` to the region's name in it, so `Sites.forIntent` builds the place to arrive at as usual. `Prompts.knownAtStart` leaves generated regions out, so they show only in the live `regionsFound` header block. Visits (`noteRegionVisit`, after moves and a journey's arrival), keeping (`noteRegionFacts` at commit, a kept place inside it) and fading (`fadeRegions`: lore and state go, name, parent and route stay) mirror sites.

**Drawn site maps** (task 6-3): `Sites.fromMap` keeps each room's `shape`, sizes a room from its area and adds "about 30 × 20 ft" to its description when the map has a grid, turns spots into features, and hands guard posts and spawn points to `checkOccupants`. `Sites.imageMap` tells the page a site has an image; `SiteDraw.renderImage` draws it with the knowledge rule decided in `ui.js` (rule 11): an SVG mask shows the picture only through known rooms. The shared library (`library/`) is loaded once by each page into `Scenario.library`; `prepareWorld` adds the maps for a world's `generatedSites.library` setting tags, marked `library: true`, and `Sites.libraryFits` prefers the world's own. Trips made companions matter: someone present whose action says they come along ("comes along", "rides with you", "follows Rook", not negated) and who sets no move goes where the player went (`Eng.comesAlong`, guard `came along`); without it the frame kept them behind and the narrator took them anyway (playtest-api-chop-shop).

**Character creator** (task 4-1, `js/charmaker.js`, before a game exists): the form's values go through `CharMaker.build`, which refuses (never clamps) skills over the slot's points or cap and returns plain-language errors, then `Scenario.characterProblems`, like an authored file. **Suggest** fills one field (traits, voice or description): `CharMaker.draftState` makes a throwaway game with the draft as the player, so the system message is the exact header the game will use (KoboldCpp's cache is warm for turn 1); the user message (`charSuggest`) holds what the player has filled in, never their secret. The kind is `worldbuild (character)`, so it routes with World building. `cleanSuggestion` keeps new short traits or one paragraph within the field's limit; the page shows the result as an offer and applies nothing until the player picks it.

**Undo and reroll** (playtest tools). `runTurn` clones the whole state before step 1; when the turn succeeds the clone (minus its own `undo`) becomes `state.undo`, so there is one level of undo. Undo swaps it back in: facts, time, clocks, rolls, any chapter the commit closed, and the previous scene pending again with all its beats (a step-in is undone too; the page re-selects it). Reroll works only on the pending turn: it puts `recent` and time/needs/clock values back to just after step 5 and re-runs steps 6–7b with the stored system prompt, so rolls, moves, events and clocks stay and only the prose, beats and settled time change. Save files leave `undo` out; the autosave keeps it.

## Prompts and the cache

`system` is identical for every call in a turn: setting, genre guidance, rules (the engine's own include telling the story impartially: no flattering, protecting or favouring the player, so a lethal world stays lethal for them too), locations, groups, cast (public sheet with pronouns), chapter summaries, and player-known facts. It only grows at the end between turns, so KoboldCpp reuses most of it (in logs, 1,800 tokens processed for the first call and only ~600–1,000 for the next two). Anything that changes per call (status, needs, relations, clocks, secrets, the scene, the task) goes in `user`. That includes how people look: the narrator's status lines carry the first sentence of `appearance` for the people in the scene only, so a cast of twenty doesn't grow every prompt.

**Blocks.** Every prompt is an ordered list of named blocks: `P.layouts.<prompt>` lists block ids, `P.blocks.<id>` builds one from the prompt's context (text, a list of lines, or `null` to leave it out), and `P.build` joins them. A new section (genre guidance, lore, regions) is one block function plus one position in a layout. In the header, only the blocks in `P.LIVE_HEADER` (`newcomers`, `chapters`, `facts`) may change during play, and they stay last (`newcomers` lists people promoted from extras, so the static cast block never changes); a test enforces this, and `tests/prompts.snapshot.test.js` pins every prompt byte for byte.

**Genre.** The `genre` block (after the setting) comes from the world's `tags` and `toneNote` through `js/genres.js`: one tone line per known tag, then what a major event means and how lethal danger is, both from the first known tag (tags are listed most important first, so `cozy, horror` is gentle dread and `horror, cozy` is lethal with warm moments). Freeform tags are named as written; a world with no tags and no tone note gets no block. It is static for a game, so it never disturbs the cache.

**Telemetry.** Each turn (and each reroll) leaves a report in `Engine.telemetry` (the last 20, in memory only): every model call with tokens in and out (from the reply's `usage`, or an estimate marked `~`), wall time, retries and salvage, plus the guards that fired. KoboldCpp doesn't report how much of the prompt it reused, so for KoboldCpp backends the report shows prompt processing time from `/api/extra/perf` after each call: a reused prefix processes in a fraction of the time. Providers that report `prompt_tokens_details.cached_tokens` get a cached column. Debug shows the reports as tables, and "Copy turn report" gives plain text for pasting into a chat.

**Recordings.** Debug › Record starts `E.Recorder` (`js/recorder.js`): from a copy of the state, it wraps `E.rng` and `LLM.transport` and keeps, per action (turn, reroll, undo, marked by the engine), every random number drawn and every model request and reply. The export is a replay fixture (`tests/fixtures/README.md`); `tests/replay.js` plays it back with the same dice and replies and fails on any different call. No keys or URLs are recorded.

**Guards.** Whenever the engine overrules or cleans up the model it records `{ stage, type, detail }` in the turn's `res.guards`: `move cancelled`, `reveal blocked`, `leak deflected`, `event dropped` (resolve); `agenda step` (resolve); `harm`, `harm rejected`, `crisis ignored`, `montage dropped`, `region tag faded`, `body gives out`, `reckoning`, `ask ignored`, `sidelined` (resolve); `id tag replaced`, `speaker label removed`, `retold last scene`, `cut-off paragraph dropped`, `cut to last sentence` (a line of dialogue it stops in is closed), `repeated paragraph removed`, `player ending trimmed` (a closing paragraph that opens on the player but hands over to someone by name, or he/she, and doesn't come back to "you" is kept), `relocation regenerated`, `continuation dropped` (scene; Reroll replaces these); `update rejected`, `update failed`, `cast tier`, `agenda set`, `agenda rejected` (memory, recorded at the next turn's commit and reported with that turn, tagged with the scene's turn). New guards should call `guard()` in `engine.js` so they show up in reports.

Budgets scale with `E.memoryContext()`: the smallest context among the backends routing uses, capped by `memoryCap` (default 32K). Facts ~20%, chapter summaries ~12%, lore ~10% (in the user message). Weight-3 facts are always kept; minor ones go first.

## Cast tiers

Task 2-1. Every non-player character is **local** or **secondary**; the player is always local.

- **Local**: the full simulation. Present locals get an intent; offstage locals get the OFFSTAGE brief (where, want, secret) in the intent call. Needs tick at the world's rates.
- **Secondary**: in the background. No intent, one line in the intent call's BACKGROUND block (`id (Name, role), in Place: wants …`, want cut to 60 characters, never the secret), so adding people adds one short line each and richer sheets add nothing. Needs settle as in downtime routine. Events can still move them and give them private facts.
- **Start**: authored `tier` or `pinned` on the cast entry, else local when in the player's starting room.
- **Promotion** (engine only): present at the start of a turn or named in the player's action (`mentions`, before the intent call and before the turn's snapshot, so a step-in keeps it); at commit, anyone in the player's room after the update step and anyone in a seen or heard event (none after a step-in: the event may not have reached the player; hidden events never). Each also sets `lastSeen`.
- **Demotion** at commit: non-pinned offstage locals with `turn − lastSeen ≥ demoteAfter` (default 4); then, if more than `maxLocal` (default 6) locals are away from the player, the longest-unseen non-pinned ones. Settings: the world's `tiers`, over `Eng.TIERS`.
- **Cache**: the header's cast block lists everyone the same way whatever their tier, so a tier change never disturbs the cached prefix; all tier-dependent detail is in the intent user message. The downtime skip still briefs everyone (its positions need the whole cast).
- **Debug**: every change is a `cast tier` guard (resolve stage for promotions before the intent call, memory stage at commit), and the characters view shows each tier, pin and `lastSeen`. The sidebar lists you, the people with you and other locals, then secondary people in a collapsed **Elsewhere** list.
- `Eng.cast(state, { tier })` filters by tier; `Eng.tier(state, id)` reads one.

## Agendas

Task 2-2. A non-player character's own goal, as 1–5 steps at a pace (`hours` per step). `null`, or a goal like "keep routine" with no steps, is routine: nothing fires.

- **Advancing** (engine only, like clocks): during turns, `advanceTime` moves only **secondary** characters' agendas (`progress += hours / agenda.hours`); local ones pause, because they act through intents. A step reaching 1 is **due** and waits there until it is written. The time snapshots that wind a short scene's clock back (and Reroll) carry agendas too.
- **Due steps become events**: the next intent call's user message lists them under AGENDA STEPS DUE, each asking for one event tagged `"agenda": id` (outside the local event cap). `applyAgendaEvents` takes the first tagged event per due step, drops others (`event dropped`), and writes the step itself ("Sorensen runs a second culture…") when the model left it out. A `hidden` agenda's event is always hidden (a private fact for that person); a `heard` one is heard (or seen in the player's room). A **contact** step moves the person into the player's room and is seen; the commit's tier update then makes them local. Every completed step is an `agenda step` guard. A step-in rewinds the turn, so the step is due again and is written the next turn.
- **Context**: the step someone is on appears only in the intent call (PRIVATE for present people, the OFFSTAGE brief, `now:` on a background line, cut to 50 characters), never in the header or the narrator's prompt.
- **Proposals**: at commit, the first living secondary character with no `agenda` field at all (or a finished agenda that hasn't had its follow-up) gets one `intent (agenda)` call: their role, traits, want, secret and a few private facts in the user message, asking for `{goal, steps (2–4), hours, contact_step}` or `{"routine": true}`. One call per turn at most. `cleanAgenda(raw, true)` validates it (hours clamped to 2–72, proposals always hidden); anything invalid, or a failed call, is routine (`agenda rejected`), and a good one is `agenda set`. A follow-up is offered once, then the person settles into routine. Saves from before 2-2 load with `agenda: null`, so they never start proposal calls.
- **Downtime**: see below. Debug's characters view shows each agenda's goal, step, progress and whether it is due.

## Extras

Task 2-3. Minor people kept as stubs in `state.extras` (`{id, name, role, pronouns, location|null, note}`; groups are standees since task 3-2), outside `state.order`, so the cast helpers, tiers, cards and needs never see them.

- **Prompts**: never the header. The intent call lists the ones with the player (with their notes) and the rest on one line (ids, so events can use them); the `now` block and the narrator's frame give name and role only, never the note; the update step's valid ids list them. The scene bar shows who is with you.
- **Events and facts**: event `who` and `moves` may use extras; facts may be `about` them or `known_by` the ones present (`ctx.extrasPresent`). The update step may move an extra under the same "seen in the scene" rule; moods, tags, needs, relations and `player_attitude` toward them are rejected.
- **New ones**: only when the world sets `inventExtras`. The intent and update replies may carry `new_extras` (`{name, role, location, group}`); `Eng.proposeExtra` allows one a turn and 30 in all, makes the id, and treats a name already in use as that person (`extra added` / `extra rejected`). A proposed group becomes a standee in a room.
- **Promotion**: an individual named in the player's action on two different turns, or `about` a fact of weight 2+, is due. At commit, at most once a turn, a `worldbuild` call (its own routing step, "World building"; Hybrid sends it external) gets the stub, every fact about them and the last two scenes that mention them (so the sheet matches how they were shown), and returns a sheet. `Eng.characterFromExtra` keeps the id, name and place, clamps skills (0–4) and relations (±30, cast ids only), defaults the guard, validates any agenda, and runs `Scenario.characterProblems` (the same checks as character files). They join the cast as local with `joined: turn`, listed in the header's `newcomers` block. A bad sheet or failed call leaves them an extra (`promotion rejected`), with one retry.
- **Bundled**: Ares Deep has Dana Ruiz (CAPCOM, a voice in Ops) and no invented extras; the holdout has Mrs. Albee and allows invented ones.

## Standees

Task 3-2. Unnamed groups (guards, a gang, the dead) as one entity in `state.standees`: `{id, label, count, location, state, faction, post, patrol, patrolHours, note}`. Groups exist only on the local map: a group that is wiped out (count 0) or flees leaves the map and is folded back into its faction (Debug says so). `Eng.tokens(state, loc)` lists every token on a room (the player and living characters, then extras, then standees) for the map view and prompts.

- **Prompts**: never the header. The `now` block says "Unnamed groups here" (label, count, state); the intent call has an UNNAMED GROUPS block with ids and notes for the ones here and one line for the rest; the narrator's frame lists the groups around as "exactly these, no more", and gets one outcome line per group plus a `CHANGED:` line for everything the engine changed. The update step lists them with the three fields it may change.
- **Engaged groups** (`Eng.engagedStandees`): the groups in the player's room, plus groups in a linked room that the player's own words name ("sneak past the stragglers" from the dock). Only they get intents, can be a roll's target, and can have their state or room changed by the update step from outside the scene's rooms (`res.engaged`, passed to the commit). The narrator's frame lists them too.
- **Group intents**: the intent reply's `group_intents` holds one entry per engaged group (`{id, action, visible, move_to}`). Unknown, absent, repeated or `down` groups are dropped (`standee rejected`). Every group move, whether from an intent, an event or the update step, is one linked step (`connects`, either direction), and a group on a `post` holds it unless hostile (`standee move`).
- **Rolls**: the player's intent may set `against` (an engaged group that can resist). An action that sneaks ("sneak", "creep", "keep low", "slip past"…) with an able group in the player's room or a linked room always rolls stealth, normal difficulty, when the model asked for no roll (`roll required`). The DC rises by state (calm 0, alert +2, hostile +4) plus size (2–3 +1, 4–7 +2, 8–19 +3, 20+ +4). A stealth roll (`Eng.STEALTH_SKILLS`) in a world with groups also counts who could notice: each alert or hostile group in the player's room, a linked room or the destination adds +2 (at most +4); nobody on the route gives −2. The reason shows in the roll line and as a `difficulty` guard.
- **Failed stealth**: the nearest group that could notice gets warier (calm → alert → hostile); one that turns hostile next door and holds no post comes into the player's room. Each change is a `noticed` guard and a `CHANGED:` line for the narrator.
- **Update step**: `standee:<id>` with `count` (a delta; it only rises when one of this turn's events lists the group in `who`, then by at most half again), `state` (seen in the scene; `fled` removes the group) and `location` (one linked step, seen leaving).
- **Patrols** move one room an in-game hour, round the loop, while calm or alert (`advanceTime`, skips included).
- **Singling one out**: the player's intent may set `single_out: {standee, name, role}`. It is accepted only when the player's own words name the group (a word of its label), so the model can't decide it for them. The count drops by one and an individual extra (`from: 'standee'`) stands in the same room; the usual promotion path can make them a character. The last one singled out ends the group.
- **Bundled**: the holdout has `stragglers` (5 dead, calm) in the parking lot. Ares Deep has no crowds.

## Local map

Task 3-3. The model describes, the engine draws. `js/layouts.js` (DOM-free) turns the rooms into rectangles on a grid: `Layouts.plan(locations, {type, seed})` places each level breadth-first from a hub (the biggest room for `hall`, a long corridor for `corridor`, the best-linked room for `generic`), preferring spots that share a wall with every placed room it links to, and gives each link its spot on the shared wall (or `remote`: a dashed line on the same level, a "↕ hatch to …" note across levels). Levels stack top to bottom, ground floor first. It is seeded by the world and start, never random, and uses only authored data, so it is the same every time, survives saves without being stored, and never moves when people do.

- **What the player knows**: `state.seenRooms` (authored rooms start seen unless `seen: false`; entering a room, or standing next to it through an `open` link, reveals it) and `state.known[id] = {loc, turn}`, where the player last saw each character, extra and group. `Eng.noteSeen` updates both after a turn is played and again at commit (a step-in rewinds to the turn's start first): everyone in the player's room, visible moves (`outcomes`, `groups`), seen or heard events, groups in view, and people the update step moved who were in the scene. Covert moves and hidden events never update it. `Eng.knownWhere` returns `{loc, turn, now}`.
- **The cards follow the same rule**: a card says where you last saw someone ("last seen in the Office (turn 3)"), never where they really are.
- **The view**: plain SVG in theme colours. Rooms with walls, labels, features and a mark per link kind; unseen rooms dark with "?"; tokens (portraits or monograms, a counted badge for a group in its state's colour, a ring for you), faded for anyone not in view. A text list of rooms and who is known to be there is there for screen readers. Clicking a room fills in "Go to the …", a person "Talk to …", a group "Watch the …"; nothing is sent. A start's `localMap` draws its image with room hotspots instead.
- **Layout**: on desktop the story area switches between Text, Split (story and map side by side, the default) and Map, remembered per world. On a phone the 🗺 button swaps the story for the map.

**World and regional views** (task 3-4). (As built in 3-4, before the zooming map below replaced the switch:) in worlds with regions the Map pane had a Local / Region / World switch. The world view draws the world's `map` image with a polygon or point per region (`Layouts.regionAnchors` places labels, and regions left off the map along the bottom), or, without an image, a node map from `neighbours` (`Layouts.regionMap`: a fixed-step force layout seeded by the world, boxes never overlapping). Regions are filled with their controller's colour (theme variables `--f1`…`--f8`, picked by the faction's `color` or a hash of its id; no one: a dashed neutral), with up to two tag badges and your region outlined with a pin. Picking a region (click, tap or Enter) shows its public state in an info strip: name, who holds it, status, tags, the lore summary, and what changed this turn. All regions show, since region state is public (hidden events change nothing). The regional view is the same map, centred on your region at twice its size within the box of its neighbours, with rooms placed by `map.locations` and the rest of your region's rooms listed. Each region effect stamps `state.regionChanged[id] = {turn, what}`; a region changed this turn pulses once (not with reduced motion). Drag to pan, wheel or pinch to zoom, or use + − ⟲; plain SVG viewBox maths, kept per view until reset or a new game.

**The zooming map** (R-3) replaced the Local / Region / World switch. `MapDraw.level(sc, id)` (DOM-free) gives one level: the nearest `map` up the chain is its picture (the world `map` is the root's), drawn with everything outlined on it (`geometry` of the regions inside the picture's owner), the viewBox zoomed to the level (`regionBox` of what's inside it; a leaf framed by `focusBox` with what's near it, as the regional view was) and regions outside it dimmed; with no picture, a node map of its children (`Layouts.regionMap`), whose edges are routes lifted to those children (a route between regions inside two children joins them; a kind other than a border wins; `kinds` per edge). The page keeps the level per world in `ensemble.mapview` (`local`, `root` or a region id; `world` and `region` from before map to the root and the player's region), draws a breadcrumb (`MapDraw.levelPath`) down to the room, **Where I am** and **Whole world**, zooms into regions that have a level (`MapDraw.hasLevel`) on click and picks the rest, and keeps a pan/zoom viewBox per level. Closed connections on a node map are those whose every route has an unmet `requires` (`Engine.routesFrom`). Under the local map, "Ways to other places" adds the region's routes as travel buttons whose words the engine reads as setting out ("I drive to Ridge."), shown only where the trip can lead somewhere (the world builds places, or a room or place is known there); a closed one is disabled with its reason, a risky one marked. A transit room shows a travel card (on, or back) instead of a plan. The picture is always the first element of the SVG.

**Presence and unknown regions** (R-5). `state.presence` ({group: {region: 0–1}}, from the world's `presence`; save version 15) is read through `Engine.presenceIn` (strongest first, words from `PRESENCE_WORDS`, contested when the top two are both ≥ 0.25 and within 0.2) and `presenceText`; it goes in the user message's region lines (never the header), the sidebar, the info strip and the map (`contested` class). The `presence` effect sets one group's strength in a region, clamped to 0–1 and to ±`PRESENCE_STEP` (0.5) of what it was. A region's `known: false` lives in `state.regions[id].known` (missing means known, so old saves need nothing); `Engine.regionKnown` checks the region and its ancestors and `hiddenRegions` lists them all. Hidden regions are filtered out of `nearbyRegions`, `routesFrom`, seeds, effect ids, lore selection, new places (`Sites.wanted`, `regionHeadedFor`), generated starts, the holder summary and every map level (`MapDraw.level(sc, id, {hidden})`); the static header outline uses `Prompts.knownAtStart`. The intent and downtime intent calls get a `hiddenPlaces` block (rule 6). `Engine.revealRegion` reveals a region and its hidden ancestors (guard `region revealed`): through the `reveal` effect, `revealAround` (the player is inside it, or knows a fact naming it; at the start of a turn and after moves) and public events (their region, or `revealNamed` on their text). Found regions show in the `regionsFound` header block, the first of `Prompts.LIVE_HEADER`.

## Site rules

Task 3-6. The engine plays by the map that 3-3 draws.

- **Link state**: `state.links["a|b"] = {locked, open}` for every link that isn't an open way (a `locked door` starts locked; doors, hatches, windows and vents start shut). `Eng.linkInfo` gives a link as it is now (with the authored `key` holders), `Eng.linkShown` how the map draws it (an unlocked locked door is a door; an open one is a gap). Shut links block sight (`Eng.visibleRooms`).
- **Movement follows links**: `Eng.route(state, from, to, {who})` goes only through ways that person can pass. A move with no way through is cancelled (`move blocked`): the player's own (the narrator is told "but the door between … is locked, so you stay in …"), other characters' intents, event moves, the update step, downtime positions and groups. A move through rooms records them (`via`), and the narrator says "through …".
- **Routes between regions** (R-2): a move whose rooms are in different regions also follows region routes (`Eng.tripBetween` → `Eng.regionTrip`: a shortest-time search over `routeEdges`, which are the regions' routes both ways unless `oneWay`, plus a free border between siblings where either has none; starts are the region and its ancestors not shared with the goal, goals the destination and its own such ancestors, so a route on a region serves what's inside it). A closed leg (`requires` not holding, by `Timeline.holds`) blocks the move with its reason (`move blocked`); a risky one makes the player's move a hard roll on the route's skill (`roll required`; `crossed` on a success, `turned back` and with `failHarm` one step of `Eng.harm` on a failure). Non-player moves can't cross closed legs or trips longer than the step. A trip longer than the step that the player's words don't set out on is cancelled (`move cancelled`). A trip with time gives the narrator the way ("by road to Ridge (45m)").
- **Journeys** (R-2): after the intent call (which resolves where the player is going) and any site build, `Eng.journeyFor` checks for an open (or risky and rolled) trip longer than the step that the player's words set out on (`PLAYER_MOVES`, travel verbs, or naming the place). Then the turn rewinds to its start copy (which already has a built site) and runs `skipTurn` with `{journey}`: the plan is the skip's (`planMajorSkip`) cut to the trip's hours as reason `arrive`, unless a stop comes first. `frame.journey` and `frame.arrival` tell the downtime intent and the narrator; `endJourney` puts the player and named companions at the destination, or in a transit room (`transit: true`, its own site, outdoor, the region the trip reached, trip links to the destination and back) that `Eng.clearTransit` removes at the start of a later turn once no one is in or last known in it. The set-aside intent call shows in Debug (`journey`). Journeys don't count toward the skip streak.
- **Locks**: the player's intent may set `unlock` (a room next to them across a locked door); it always rolls (mechanics, else the world's first skill) unless the player holds the key, and opens on a success. A key holder's `unlock`/`lock` needs no roll; anyone else's is rejected (`lock rejected`). Key holders present are told so in PRIVATE.
- **Doors, features, rooms in the update step**: `link:a|b` `open` true/false (only in the scene's rooms, never a locked one), `feature:room|name` `state` (only features with a state, in the scene's rooms), `room:id` `seen` true (a plan or a description reveals it). The prompt lists only the ways and features next to the player and the unknown rooms.
- **Sneaking**: a stealth roll counts every room on the player's route and the rooms next to them; cameras that are on and watched entrances on the way add +2 each with wary groups (at most +4). The −2 for an empty route applies in any world with watchers of some kind.
- **Inferred features**: the player's intent may carry `new_feature` `{name, to}` for something the map is silent about. Accepted only in the player's room, when the player's own words use it, not over an authored feature, up to 8 per room. A way out (`to`) needs an outside wall in the layout (`Layouts.hasOutsideWall`), a target that is outside or shares a wall, and no secure room at either end; it becomes a shut link (a window, vent, hatch or door by its name). Layouts are placed from the authored rooms (`sc.locations`) and only draw links from the live ones, and the header's LOCATIONS block lists the authored rooms, so nothing inferred moves the map or changes the header; the live ways and things are in the user message ("Ways from here", "Things here").
- **Feature relocation**: `findRelocation` also catches the player reaching a feature that only a room they aren't in has ("You're almost to the abandoned pickup", playtest-stragglers-sneak), and, after a move, walking back out of the room they moved to ("You slip out of the office", playtest-office-window). It is given the rooms the player actually passed through.
- **Ways out from the player's words**: when the model moves the player but leaves `new_feature` out, and the action names a way out ("window", "vent", "skylight", "hatch", "fire escape"), the engine proposes it itself, in the room the words name or else the player's room (`Eng.wayFromWords`). A way out may be in a room on the player's way this turn: the player walks there first and the move goes through the new way (`via`).

## Memory

- **Facts** are short sentences with names, an owner list (`known_by`) and a weight. The update step can't record a fact again that the same people already know (75% of the content words shared), and can't change a world value a clock set earlier in the same turn. Facts the player could not have seen or heard are recorded as known only by whoever did. The engine drops anyone the update step names who wasn't there: a fact can be known only by the player, the people present, those in the turn's events (hidden ones included), or `all`.
- **Chapters** close every N turns (default 5), early on a big moment (after at least 2 turns), when facts outgrow their budget, and after a major-event skip. Minor player-known facts are folded into a 2–4 sentence journal entry; weight-3 facts and private facts are kept individually.
- **Mood and tags** (task 1-6). The update step may set a non-player character's `mood` (how they come across, ≤ 60 characters) and add or remove `tags` (short visible states, ≤ 32 characters and 5 words, lasting `turns`, default 6). The engine rejects moods for the player, moods that overlap the character's secret (the card shows them), and feelings or need names as tags; merges a tag that shares a content word with an existing one (names excluded); keeps at most 4, dropping the oldest expiring one; and removes expired tags when the next turn commits. Changes and expiries show in Debug as `tags & mood`. Authored tags (and old `conditions`) last. Derived tags ("on edge", "hurt") are computed from needs and health for the card and never stored. Moods and tags go in the user message's status lines, never the header.
- **The player's feelings** are a separate tally, changed only by `player_attitude` judged from the player's own words (±10 per turn), with a note of why, or by the player's own correction on a card (`Eng.correctAttitude`, ±5 a step, an optional why that replaces the note; logged in `state.corrections` and Debug, recorded as a `correct` action). A correction made while a turn is pending is kept on `pending.corrections` and re-applied if the player steps in (which rewinds to the turn's start). Undo goes back to before the last action, so it drops corrections made since then. Longer time steps tell the narrator to keep the player's routine consistent with those feelings.


A fact whose `about` is only the player is something they did, so the player is always among its knowers (the update step kept giving "Jordan knocked on the office door" only to Dex and Ruth). A fact that also names someone else ("Okafor suspects Kit…") keeps the knowers it was given, so private suspicions about the player stay private.
## Lore

Task 5-1, `js/lore.js`. Lore entries (format: `docs/WORLDS.md` › Lore) let a big world stay playable in 8K: the world carries broad context, regions the heaviest detail, and only what matters for the scene goes in.

- **Always-on** world entries are static, so they go in the header (`loreAlways`, after groups and before the cast; also in the generator's header). Region `lore` summaries stay in the REGIONS block.
- **Everything else** is chosen per call by `Lore.select(state, {action})` and goes in the user message (`lore` block) of the intent, scene, continuation and both downtime prompts, never the update or suggestion calls. It is chosen where the player is at the time of the call, so a move in the turn swaps the destination's room and region lore into the scene.
- **Triggers**, in rank order: the player's room and the people present (the player included); the player's region; a keyword in the player's action; a keyword in the last scene. Within a trigger: `priority` (high first), then level (location, character, region, topic, world), then the order written. An entry triggered twice appears once, with both reasons. Always-on entries are never repeated.
- **Keywords** match whole words and phrases, ignoring case, with letters and digits of any script as word characters (`ash` is not in `crash`; `dust storm` matches `DUST  storm`).
- **Budget**: `Memory.budgets().lore`, about 10% of the memory context. Entries are taken best first; one that doesn't fit is skipped, and a smaller one later can still go in.
- **Cache**: the selection never touches the header, so the header stays identical for every call in a turn whatever the player types (tested).
- **Lore is public.** The narrator sees it, so `validateWorld` refuses an entry that shares 4+ content words with someone's secret (pool or start cast). Future events belong in the canon timeline.
- **Debug**: each turn report lists the entries each call included and why (`Lore.collector`, filled by the `lore` block while a report is open), and "Copy turn report" includes them.
- **Creator**: renaming a region moves its lore entries' `ref` (inline or in `lore/` files); deleting it turns them into topics, keeping the text.

## Secrets

Each secret-keeper has a `guard`:

| guard | cracks when |
|---|---|
| low | confronted directly, stress ≥ 0.6, or trust ≥ 30 |
| medium | stress ≥ 0.85, trust ≥ 50, or confronted while stress ≥ 0.6 |
| high | stress ≥ 0.95 or trust ≥ 70 |

"Stress" here is the highest of the needs listed in the world's `revealPressure` (default `stress` and `fear`); a world without those needs cracks secrets only through trust, confrontation (low guard) or a social roll. A successful social roll works on anyone. A reveal attempt is either the `reveals` field (any truthy value) or a leak: 3+ content words shared with the secret, or a confession verb plus 1+. Unearned attempts become "is pressed about it but keeps the secret".

## Time

| step | span | narration | events |
|---|---|---|---|
| a few minutes | 15 min | 2–3 short paragraphs (60–120 words), beat by beat | usually none |
| about an hour | about 1 h (follows the scene) | 3–5 paragraphs (100–200), key exchange live, rest compressed | 0–1 |
| a few hours | up to 3 h (follows the scene) | 4–6 paragraphs (150–250), routine allowed | 1–2 |
| until next morning | to wake hour | 5–7 paragraphs (200–300), summary then live morning | 1–3 |
| until something major happens | to the next clock, random major event, or horizon | a short paragraph a beat (250–350), day-stamped montage then live crisis | up to 2 + the interrupting event |

Lengths are asked as short paragraphs of 2–4 sentences, with the words in brackets: the local 12B wrote about twice the words asked for and kept hitting the token limit (playtest-r2-drive-to-ridge), and it follows a paragraph count better. When a scene still hits the limit (`finish: length`), its cut paragraph is dropped (`cut-off paragraph dropped`) so the scene ends on a complete one; a one-paragraph scene is cut to its last sentence instead. The longer the step, the more the narrator may fill in for the player, but never big decisions. Scenes are written as beats with time markers (`[+0m]`, `[+40m]`, `[+3d]`); the page reveals them one at a time, offering **Step in here** or **Next ▸** after each. **Act** stays greyed out until the scene is read to the end or a step-in point is chosen, so the player never cuts a scene short by accident (they can type ahead).

## Events

- **Local**: people in and around the scene (an intercom call, someone arriving, an offstage character pursuing a secret).
- **Regional**: the surroundings (terrain, weather, nearby places). Default about one per 18 in-game hours on Mars, 4-hour cooldown.
- **World**: broad shifts far away (money, politics, disasters). Default about one per 96 hours, 24-hour cooldown.

The rates above are engine defaults. A world's genre tags can set others (`js/genres.js`: `cozy` is about one regional event every 72 hours, `war` every 12), and a world's own `eventRates` and `majorEventDays` always win; `Genres.pacing` settles each value in that order. The engine decides when bigger events are due; the model only writes them, guided by `eventSeeds`. Effects can change world status, a group's stance, clock values, add a new clock (at most 5 active), or change a region: `control` (it changes hands, to a group or no one), `add_tag` / `remove_tag` (up to 6 tags) and `region_status`. Each effect is one entry in the `EFFECTS` table in `engine.js` (keyed by the payload field: `world`, `groups`, `clocks`, `new_clock`, `control`, `add_tag`, `remove_tag`, `region_status`); a handler validates its own item and returns a news line or a rejection. Rejected items and unknown effect fields show in Debug as "effect rejected"; a new effect is a new table entry plus its line in the prompts' effect shape. Visibility is seen (in the player's room), heard (a sound, call or message) or hidden (private knowledge for those involved; changes nothing public).

**Regions** (task 3-1). The world's `regions` are static (name, neighbours, lore) and go in the header's REGIONS block; their changing part (controller, tags, status) is `state.regions` and goes in the user message for the player's region (the region of their room) and what's near it only, so a change of control never touches the header. **The tree** (R-1): `parent` makes regions a tree. The pure helpers are in `Scenario` (`regionChainIn`, `regionChildrenIn`, `regionNearIn`, `regionLabel`), built on every call because the creator edits regions in place, and the engine wraps them: `Engine.regionChain` (nearest first), `regionChildren`, `regionNear` (neighbours, siblings under a real parent, children; capped at `Scenario.REGION_NEAR` = 8), `nearbyRegions` (the player's region plus `regionNear`), `regionsAround` (adds the chain: where a regional event may be and which seeds apply) and `regionHolder` (who holds a region in words, summarising a parent's children when it has no controller of its own). The header outline (`Prompts.regionOutline`) walks out from the start's region over neighbour and parent–child links: the start's chain, everything within two steps, the chain's siblings and `notable` regions in full (24 at most, in the world's order), the rest as a "Further away" names line (60 at most, top level first); a line gains ", a <label> in <parent name>" (`Prompts.regionWhat`) only when it has them, so flat worlds keep their lines. The user message gives `Region:`, `Inside:` (one entry per ancestor) and `Nearby:` (capped), so its size follows depth, never the tree's size. Lore picks region entries for the player's region (group 1) and its ancestors (after keywords in the action, nearest first). New places (task 4-4) may be in the player's region, an ancestor or a near region. The state shape is unchanged, so there is no save migration. **Routes** (R-2): `Scenario.deriveRoutes` turns `neighbours` into `border` routes when composing (and in save migration 13 → 14, which also renames site links from `route` to `trip`); `Scenario.routeOf` fills a route from its kind (`Scenario.ROUTE_KINDS`, the world's `routeKinds`). "Near" follows routes (own, then incoming two-way, then neighbours). The user message's `Routes from here:` lists the region's explicit routes that aren't a plain zero-time border (`Engine.routesFrom`), with why a closed one is closed; flat worlds' prompts don't change. Regional and world events carry a `region`: an unknown id is reported as `event region` and a regional event falls back to the player's region; a world event may have none. It is kept on the news item and in Debug. Regional `eventSeeds` tied to a region away from the player are left out. Effects may change any region, near or far, so the world shifts around the story; the prompt lists every region id for effects, but only nearby regions' current state. Only events change regions; the update step can't.

## Canon timeline

Task 5-2, `js/timeline.js` (format: `docs/WORLDS.md` › Canon timeline). Dated events that happen whatever the player does, unless the story broke them. Rule 1 throughout: the model never judges whether an event can happen.

- **The past** (before the start) is seeded at New game (`Timeline.seed`): public facts tagged with the event id (`fact.canon`) and a chapter-zero summary, "Before the story", at the head of STORY SO FAR. Those facts are marked `inSummary` and left out of the facts block (`Memory.headerFacts`), so they cost tokens once; they still count for `known` and for what the player knows. Hidden past events become private facts for `who`. Past effects are never applied.
- **Due events** (`Timeline.due(state, hours)`): before the intent call, every pending event dated within the step, with its preconditions checked once against the start-of-turn state. As written: applied after the model's events (effects through the `EFFECTS` table, a public weight-3 fact, news, and an EVENT line for the narrator). Broken with `drop`: nothing public, guard `canon dropped` with what broke. Broken with `diverge`: the intent prompt gets the original text, what broke in words, and a `canon` field; the reply's version is applied like the original (unknown ids `canon rejected`; missing or leaking a secret: dropped, `canon diverge missing`). Hidden events change nothing public. Outcomes live in `state.canon[id]`.
- **Rationing**: canon events are outside it. They don't need `tiersDue`, don't count toward the event caps and don't touch `tierLast`, so random regional and world events keep their own rhythm.
- **Step in here** rewinds to the turn's start (rule 8), `state.canon` included, so an event fired in that turn comes due again.
- **Never early**: future events reach no prompt before they are due (not the header, not projection), so the narrator never knows the future.
- **Projected events**: when no pending event falls in the next `projectTimeline.days` (default 3), one `worldbuild (timeline)` call (routed with World building, the turn's header as its system message) asks for 1–`max` likely public events in that window, given where things stand and the resolved timeline with its usual spacing. `Timeline.checkProjected` keeps only those dated in the window, with real regions and known effect keys, no secret (4+ content words), no one dead, and no repeat of a planned event; they go in `state.timeline` and play like canon events (no preconditions). After any attempt it waits 6 in-game hours, so a failing backend isn't asked every turn. Games saved before this task (`projection: false`) never project.
- **Debug** has a Timeline section: upcoming events (projected ones marked) with each precondition ✓ or ✗ as it stands, then what became of the rest. Guards: `canon fired`, `canon dropped`, `canon diverged`, `canon diverge missing`, `canon rejected`, `timeline planned`, `timeline rejected`, `timeline failed`.

## Research helper

Build time only (`tools/research.mjs`, logic in `js/research.js`): play never fetches anything, sends no place names anywhere and never waits on a fetch; a place the story reaches without lore is handled from the model's own knowledge, as generated sites are. The command loads the game's engine in Node, so it calls the local KoboldCpp or an external API through `js/llm.js` exactly as the game does (routed as World building; an API key comes only from `ENSEMBLE_API_KEY`), and it can read wiki pages without a browser's cross-site limits.

- **What to research comes from the world, not from crawling.** `plan` writes `research.json` in the world folder: a topic for the world, each region, group and character. The creator trims it (`skip`), or points a topic at a `page`, a `url` or pasted `text`. Lore is for what the model doesn't know or would get wrong about this world; general knowledge is never researched, and links are never followed.
- **Sources**: a MediaWiki API (`--wiki`, Wikipedia by default; Fandom and other MediaWiki wikis too): search, then the page's plain text (TextExtracts, or the parsed HTML with the markup stripped where a wiki lacks it), cut at the reference sections. A URL is fetched and stripped the same way.
- **One chunk per call** (about a third of the research backend's context, at most 3000 tokens; at most 4 chunks a page, the start matters most). The system message is the generator header (`genHeader`); the user message has the topic, the start (`asOf`, and the start's real date when given), the world's ids and the chunk (`P.research`). Optional `--check-known` first asks the model what it knows and skips the topic when its answer has 70% of the names and numbers in the source's opening.
- **Checks** (`Research.check`, rule 1; a problem is named in the next try through `StartGen.attempt`): an entry sharing a run of 8 words with the chunk is sent back (Wikipedia and most fan wikis are CC BY-SA), as is one over 110 words (split it); under 15 words is dropped; refs are matched to ids by id or name, and an unknown one makes the entry a topic; an entry that names nothing of this world (the topic, a region, group, person or room) is dropped; one that shares 4+ content words with a secret is dropped. Keywords keep the model's aliases plus a short title. At most `perTopic` entries (default 4), one of them about the world.
- **The start date**: things after the start come back as `later`. With the start's real date (`--date`), dated ones become timeline drafts on the world's day count; the rest, and any `secrets` found, are suggestions printed and kept in the job, never written.
- **Resuming**: the job saves after every chunk; a failed topic is marked and the run goes on; running again retries only what isn't done.
- **Output** (`Research.apply`, also `apply` alone): drafts (`"draft": true`) in `lore/research.json` (listed in `world.json`) and in `world.json`'s timeline (ids `research-…`). Re-applying replaces earlier drafts and keeps what was accepted. The game ignores drafts (`Lore.entries`, `Timeline.all`); validation still checks them. The creator's Lore and Timeline tabs mark them and offer Accept and Accept all (`LoreEdit.accept`); a lore packet from an outside tool can use the same flag to wait for review.

## Downtime skip

`planMajorSkip` simulates clocks hour by hour (following `then` chains), finds the next agenda **contact** step (anyone's) and the next timeline event, and compares them with a random major event drawn from an exponential distribution (`majorEventDays`), capped by `majorHorizonDays`. A timeline event stops the skip like a clock (`reason: 'canon'`; a clock or agenda at the same hour comes first). Its preconditions are checked when the skip reaches it: as written, it is the interrupting event and the model writes none; broken with `diverge`, the model's `major_event` is the altered version; broken with `drop`, the model picks a major event as for a random stop. Other events due at the same hour happen as news. Time advances in routine mode (hunger and fatigue settle near normal). Every living non-player agenda runs through the downtime (`Eng.runAgendas`): steps completed on the way become facts (private for hidden agendas) and are listed for the montage, and a contact step waits, due. When a contact step ends the skip (`reason: 'agenda'`), that person comes to find the player: it is the interrupting event, they end up with the player whatever the positions say, and the step completes. Due agenda steps are never written by the skip's own events. The player ends up somewhere else only if their plan names that place (`player_where` is otherwise ignored, as `move cancelled`). One model call writes the montage, any news, the interrupting event (unless a clock or an agenda caused it) and where everyone is; the narration is a day-stamped montage ending in a live scene. Choosing it means "let's see what happens", so very short skips are allowed.

**Harm, emergencies and endings** (playtest-coffee-skips). Health only gets worse through `Eng.harm` (never back from the dead; the update step can't revive anyone either), the player's by one step per blow, and the player's death ends the story (`gameOver`; a skip that kills them gets a closing scene). Harm comes from a clock's `fireHarm` (`all` or everyone `outside`), from a random or rewritten major event's `harm` list (only people it names or who are where it happens) and from an ignored emergency. A turn that ends on a clock firing, or a skip that ends on a clock or a random major event (not timeline news), leaves `state.crisis = { turn, text, who }` (`who`: everyone alive at the player's site), open for the next turn only (`Eng.openCrisis`). A normal next turn shows it to the intent call (`STILL HAPPENING`: it plays out this span whatever the player does) and to the update step (record what the people who needed the player think, in relations). A skip next turn plays it out first without the player: the model's `crisis.outcome` (or a plain line) becomes the first event and a weight-3 public fact, its `harm` applies to people at the site, and everyone it concerned trusts the player `Eng.CRISIS_TRUST` (6) less, engine-side. A random stop right after an emergency is a turning point that grows out of the people, not another disaster. Montage moments the player leads that share no word stem with their plan in their first sentence are dropped (`montage dropped`, rule 3), and so are moments that name someone and share 3+ words with their secret, or 1+ with a confession word (`leak deflected`, rule 5). A skip that ends with someone coming to talk doesn't close a chapter. Narration prompts list phrases two or more of the last four scenes share (`Eng.wornPhrases`, 4–12 words within a clause, 2+ content words that aren't names) as worn out.

**Things happen to the player too** (playtest-coffee-bystander). A skip settles the player's needs only when their plan covers them (`Eng.unmetNeeds`: an empty or routine plan covers all; otherwise each need needs its relief words, like sleep or eat); the rest climb as in a live scene. Every need has a limit (`Eng.needLimit`): fatigue, hunger, thirst, stress and fear have built-in ones (`Eng.NEED_LIMITS`), any other need gets a plain one whose relief is its own name, and a world can set any field in `needs[].limit`. Hours spent at a need's limit are kept (`needsOver`), and after the need's `grace` (fatigue 12 h, hunger 48 h, thirst 12 h) the skip stops there (`body`): the player collapses, gets one step more hurt up to the need's `worst` (hunger and thirst can kill), and the need drops back. The same limit pushed again by a body already at its `worst` goes one step further (`pc.collapses`); past `badly_hurt` only where the leading genre's danger is lethal, so a survival world's fourth night on coffee can kill. Each collapse counts as sitting things out (the others drop everything for it). The update step can't undo any of this: it never raises the player's needs, it worsens the player's health only one step and not on a turn the engine already hurt them, and it mends anyone only a step at a time, the player only when their own action rests or seeks care (`Eng.restsOrHeals`). Every emergency a skip sits out is remembered (`state.ignored`); played turns count too: the intent call says whether the action dealt with the open emergency (`player.crisis_answered`; without an answer, the action must share a word with it), and if not the engine applies the same trust drop and counts it. After `Eng.RECKONING_AFTER` (2), the person at the site who thinks least of the player comes to confront them with something real (an ultimatum, a decision made without them, something taken away): a skip stops `Eng.RECKONING_HOURS` (3) in, and a played turn brings them into the room (`SOMEONE HAS HAD ENOUGH` in the intent call). The count starts over then, or when the player deals with an emergency. Whoever ends a skip by coming to the player (an agenda's contact step, or a reckoning) leaves an ask (`state.ask`) that the next action answers by naming them or sharing a word with it; turned away, they trust the player less (`Eng.ASK_TRUST`) and it counts as sitting out. A skip's reckoning carries the model's own words for what is demanded or taken away (`reckoning`), and ignoring a reckoning sidelines the player: everyone's trust drops `Eng.SIDELINED_TRUST`, a lasting public fact, and a `sidelined by the …` tag. A random major event after the player sat an emergency out, or on the third skip in a row (`state.skipStreak`), happens where the player is and can hurt them. A model event that repeats the timeline event ending the skip is dropped. The update step sees each clock's name and that higher means closer to going off (it had put off "Stored water runs low" three times as the water ran out).

## Backends

Two backends (local and external), each a preset: KoboldCpp on this computer, KoboldCpp remote (tunnel URL), OpenRouter, or any OpenAI-compatible API. Each pipeline step (characters & events, narration, memory updates, chapter summaries, world building) is routed to one of them; "Hybrid" sends the judgment, prose and world-building steps external and keeps bookkeeping local. Keys live in browser settings, never in saves.
